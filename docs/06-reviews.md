# Gathering Book Reviews (Free)

Reviews help new readers decide whether your book is for them. You cannot buy
or fake them — but you can honestly ask readers to share their thoughts. This
document covers where, how, and when to ask, plus what to avoid.

## Where reviews matter most

1. **Amazon** — the most visible retail reviews
2. **Goodreads / StoryGraph** — discovery and reader communities
3. **Your website / hub site** — pull quotes you have permission to use
4. **Library and bookseller catalogs** — sometimes accept reader suggestions

## When to ask

- Launch week (from ARC readers and early buyers)
- After a reader messages you saying they enjoyed the book
- At local events and library talks
- In your newsletter (once per issue, gently)

## How to ask (templates)

**In your newsletter:**

> If you read [*Book Title*]([book link]) and enjoyed it, a quick review on
> Amazon or Goodreads helps more than any ad budget I could buy. Even one or
> two sentences makes a difference. Thank you.

**In the back matter of your book:**

> Did this book help you? Please consider leaving a review on Amazon or
> Goodreads. It takes two minutes and helps other beginners find the book.
>
> [Amazon review link] | [Goodreads link]

**In person (after a library talk or event):**

> "If you found this helpful, a review online helps other people discover it.
> No pressure at all — only if you want to."

## Sample review language you can suggest (readers write their own)

> "[Book Title] explained [topic] in plain English, step by step. I went from
> knowing nothing to feeling confident in a weekend. Highly recommend for
> beginners."
>
> — [First name, last initial], with permission

Always ask permission before quoting a reader by name. First name + last
initial is the safest public format.

## What to avoid (retailer policies)

- Never pay for reviews or offer gifts in exchange for positive reviews
- Never ask family members to review (many retailers remove these)
- Never write reviews for your own book under different names
- Never trade reviews ("I'll review yours if you review mine")
- Never pressure readers — "honest review" means any honest rating

## Responding to reviews

- Thank readers publicly for positive reviews (Amazon allows author comments on
  some profiles; otherwise thank them on social media with permission)
- Never respond to negative reviews. Not even to correct them. Let them stand.
- Learn from patterns in feedback for your next edition

## Pull quotes for marketing

With the reader's permission, use short quotes in:

- Your author bio and book description
- Social media posts
- The press release
- Local event flyers

Format: "[Quote]" — [First name, last initial], reader

## Privacy-safe checklist

- [ ] All reviews are honest and unpaid
- [ ] No personal data about reviewers is published
- [ ] Quotes used only with permission, first name + last initial
- [ ] No home addresses, phone numbers, or private emails shared
- [ ] No pressure or incentives for positive ratings
